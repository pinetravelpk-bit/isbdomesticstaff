Word of mouth can still work when the person recommending a worker has employed them for years. The problem is that most "recommendations" are second or third hand. What a Good Domestic Staff Agency in Islamabad Should Offer

Before you share your address or pay anything, compare the agency against this list. A reputable agency will be able to explain each point clearly.

A real screening process

Ask exactly how candidates are screened. You should hear specifics: original CNIC seen and copied, permanent and current addresses confirmed, previous employers called, and a skills interview for the role. Police character verification should be available when you need it, especially for live-in staff, drivers and watchmen.

A wide but relevant pool of workers

Good agencies maintain candidates for many roles, including maids, housekeepers, cooks, drivers, nannies, patient attendants, chowkidars, office boys, cleaners and gardeners. More importantly, they only send people who fit your requirements instead of whoever happens to be free that day.

Interviews and trials on your terms

You should be able to interview candidates at your home or at the agency office, ask practical questions and, where possible, arrange a short paid trial. An agency that pressures you to decide on the spot is protecting its fee, not your family.

Transparent charges

Fees should be explained before the search begins: what you pay, when you pay it, and what it covers. Ask whether the fee includes replacements and for how long.

After-placement support

The first month is when most problems surface. A professional agency checks in with both the family and the worker, helps resolve misunderstandings early and steps in quickly if a replacement is needed.

Quick test: Ask the agency, "What will you do if the person leaves without notice in the third week?" A confident, specific answer is a very good sign. Vague promises are a warning.

Red Flags When Choosing an Agency

Unfortunately, not everyone advertising as a domestic staff agency in Islamabad works professionally. Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

  • Full payment demanded before you meet anyone. You should see suitable candidates before committing.
  • No office address. A business that can only be reached on WhatsApp is hard to hold accountable.
  • Copies of CNIC that look edited or blurry. Ask to see the original at the interview.
  • "References" who cannot describe the worker's actual duties. Genuine former employers remember details.
  • Unrealistic promises, such as "same-day live-in nanny with 10 years' experience" for every request.
  • Pressure to keep the worker's salary low while the agency takes a monthly cut without explaining it.
  • Reluctance to put anything in writing. If terms are not written down, they can change later.

How the Hiring Timeline Usually Looks

Families often ask how long it takes to get staff through an agency. While every case is different, a typical placement in Islamabad follows this pattern:

  1. Day 1 – Requirement call. You explain the role, timings, live-in or live-out preference, family size, location (for example G-11, F-10, DHA or Bahria Town) and budget.
  2. Days 2–4 – Shortlisting. The agency checks its verified pool and contacts suitable candidates, confirming availability and salary expectations.
  3. Days 3–6 – Interviews. You meet two or three candidates. Many families invite the most promising one for a trial day.
  4. Placement day. Duties, salary, off days and notice period are agreed and written down. Documents are filed with the agency.
  5. First 30 days – Follow-up. The agency checks in, solves early issues and arranges a replacement if needed.

Common roles such as maids, cooks and drivers are often filled within a few days. Specialised roles, like an experienced newborn nanny or a trained attendant for a bedridden patient, can take longer because the agency must find the right skills, not just an available person.

What to Tell the Agency So You Get the Right Person

The quality of the match depends heavily on the brief you give. Families who share specific details usually hire faster and keep their staff longer. Be ready to discuss:

  • The number of adults, children and elderly members in the house
  • The size of the home and whether there is a lawn, upper portion or annexe
  • Exact duties, for example "cleaning and laundry, no cooking" or "two meals a day, desi and some continental"
  • Working hours, weekly off and whether occasional late evenings are expected
  • Live-in or live-out, and what accommodation and meals you will provide
  • Language, gender or age preferences that matter for your household
  • Special needs, such as caring for a diabetic parent or a child with allergies
  • A realistic salary range for the role and experience you want

Agreements, Documents and Fair Treatment

A written understanding protects everyone. Through a good domestic staff agency, you should end up with a simple agreement that covers duties, salary and payment date, working hours, weekly off and annual leave, notice period on both sides, and what happens during the trial and replacement period.

Keep copies of the worker's CNIC, a recent photograph and emergency contact details. If you hire a live-in worker, many families also register their details with the local police station, which a professional agency can guide you on.

Fair treatment is just as important as paperwork. Paying on time, providing a clean place to rest, allowing the agreed day off and speaking respectfully are the biggest reasons staff stay with a family for years rather than weeks.

Questions to Ask Candidates the Agency Sends You

The agency handles verification, but the interview is your chance to judge attitude and fit. Keep the conversation relaxed and practical. Useful questions include:

  • "Tell me about your last job. What did a normal day look like?"
  • "Why did you leave, and would your previous employer hire you again?"
  • "What would you do if a child in your care had a fever while we were out?" (for nannies)
  • "Which dishes do you cook best, and can you make them with less oil?" (for cooks)
  • "Which routes do you know well in Islamabad and Rawalpindi?" (for drivers)
  • "How do you help a patient move from bed to wheelchair safely?" (for attendants)
  • "Is there anything about this job that you are not comfortable doing?"

Listen for specific, honest answers. A candidate who admits a weakness is often more reliable than one who claims to be perfect at everything.

Common Mistakes Families Make, Even With a Good Agency

Even when you work with the best domestic staff agency in Islamabad, a few avoidable mistakes can cut a placement short:

  • Changing the job after hiring. A maid hired for cleaning should not suddenly be expected to cook and babysit as well without a conversation and a salary review.
  • Skipping the trial. One working day in your home reveals more than an hour of questions.
  • Not explaining house rules early. Phone use, guests, kitchen access and timings are easier to agree on day one than to correct later.
  • Waiting too long to raise concerns. Tell the agency about small problems early, while they can still be fixed.
  • Delaying salary. Late payment is one of the most common reasons good staff quietly start looking for another job.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safer to hire through a domestic staff agency than directly?

Generally yes. A professional agency verifies identity, contacts previous employers and remains accountable after placement, which is very difficult to achieve when hiring a stranger directly or through an informal broker.

Do I pay the agency or the worker?

Usually you pay the agency a placement or service fee and pay the worker's monthly salary directly. Ask the agency to explain its charges in writing before the search begins.

Can the agency provide live-in staff?

Yes. Live-in maids, nannies, cooks and patient attendants are commonly placed through agencies. You will need to agree on accommodation, meals, working hours and days off in advance.
