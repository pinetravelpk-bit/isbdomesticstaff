Roles Commonly Hired by International Households

Housekeeper or house manager

In larger diplomatic or executive residences, a housekeeper manages daily operations: supervising cleaners and the cook, organising supplies, preparing for official dinners and receptions, and liaising with maintenance teams. Strong English and organisational skills are essential.

Cook or chef

International families often want a cook who can switch between Pakistani dishes and international food such as roast chicken, pasta, soups, salads and baked goods. Some households require experience with specific dietary needs, such as vegetarian, gluten-free or allergy-safe cooking. For entertaining, ask whether the cook has prepared plated dinners or buffets for guests.

Driver

Drivers for diplomatic and corporate families need excellent knowledge of Islamabad and Rawalpindi routes, calm driving, punctuality and discretion. Many families also want drivers who understand security protocols, such as varying routes, checking vehicles and never sharing schedules.

Nanny

Expatriate families often look for nannies who can speak English with children, follow structured routines, support homework and understand international parenting styles, such as limits on screen time and particular approaches to discipline.

Gardener and watchman

Many residences in F-6, F-7 and E-7 have gardens and gates that need full-time attention. Watchmen in diplomatic areas must follow strict visitor procedures and coordinate with official security where applicable.

Security and Verification for Diplomatic Areas

Security is often the first concern for international households. A capable household staff provider in Islamabad should support the following:

  1. Identity verification using the original CNIC and confirmation of permanent and current addresses.
  2. Reference checks with previous employers, ideally including other international households.
  3. Police character verification, which is often required for entry to the Diplomatic Enclave and secure residential areas.
  4. Documentation support for entry passes or registration with your organisation's security office.
  5. Clear records held by the provider in case your security team needs further information.

Always check your embassy's or organisation's own requirements, as many have their own vetting procedures for household staff.

Tip: Ask your organisation's security office for its household staff requirements before you start interviewing. Sharing them with your provider at the beginning saves weeks of delay later.

Cultural Understanding on Both Sides

Successful placements depend on mutual understanding. A few points that often help international employers:

  • Religious practice: most staff will pray during the day and may fast during Ramadan. Allow time for prayers and adjust workloads during fasting hours.
  • Eid and family events: Eid is the most important holiday of the year for most staff. Plan leave well in advance and expect workers to travel home.
  • Food: discuss whether staff will cook or handle pork or alcohol. Many Muslim staff will not, and it is best to agree on this before hiring.
  • Communication style: some workers may hesitate to say "no" or admit they do not understand. Encourage questions and check understanding gently.
  • Gender considerations: some families prefer female staff for childcare or for households where a woman is often alone at home. Mention preferences clearly.

Equally, a good provider prepares staff for working in international households: punctuality, privacy, using appliances, following written routines and understanding that some expectations differ from local homes.

Contracts, Pay and Benefits in International Households

International employers often bring structured employment practices, which staff appreciate. Consider including:

  • A written contract covering duties, hours, salary, overtime, leave and notice.
  • A clear salary payment date and method, with a payslip or written record.
  • Defined overtime arrangements for evening events or travel.
  • Annual leave and public holidays, including both Eids.
  • Medical support, such as help with doctor's fees or a basic health arrangement.
  • An end-of-posting bonus or reference letter, which helps the worker secure the next position.

Salaries vary with role, experience, language skills and household size. Ask your provider for current market ranges rather than relying on figures passed between expatriate groups, which can quickly become outdated.

Official Entertaining and Events at Home

Diplomatic and senior corporate households in Islamabad often host receptions, national day events and working dinners at the residence. These events place special demands on household staff:

  • Planning: agree on menus, guest numbers and timings at least a week ahead with your cook and housekeeper.
  • Extra help: large events may need additional cooks, waiters and cleaners for the day. A household staff provider in Islamabad can usually arrange experienced temporary helpers.
  • Presentation: brief staff on table settings, serving order and dress code, such as clean uniforms or simple formal wear.
  • Security: share guest lists with the watchman and coordinate with your security team for arrivals and parking.
  • After the event: plan clean-up and allow staff extra rest the following day.

Preparing Your Residence Before Staff Start

New arrivals in Islamabad often move into a furnished residence and hire staff in the same week. A little preparation makes the first days easier for everyone:

  1. Check that staff quarters are clean, furnished and have working fans, heaters and water.
  2. Stock basic cleaning supplies, kitchen essentials and uniforms if you provide them.
  3. Test appliances such as the washing machine, oven, water motor and UPS, so you can show staff how they work.
  4. Prepare a written welcome sheet with house rules, routines and emergency contacts in English and Urdu.
  5. Register staff with building or compound security before their first day.

Handover Between Postings

One of the biggest challenges for international families is what happens to good staff when a posting ends. Planning a handover protects your staff and helps the incoming family:

  1. Tell your staff about your departure date as early as possible.
  2. Write a detailed reference describing their duties, strengths and years of service.
  3. Introduce them to incoming colleagues or families who may need staff.
  4. Ask your household staff provider to help place them with a suitable new household.
  5. Settle all dues and any end-of-service bonus before you leave.

A household staff provider in Islamabad that keeps records of past placements can often match a departing family's staff with a new arrival within days. Incoming families also benefit: inheriting experienced, trusted staff who already know the city and international routines is often the smoothest start to a new posting.

Returning Overseas Pakistani Families

Families moving back to Islamabad after years abroad face a unique adjustment. Children may be used to doing more themselves, parents may be unfamiliar with current salary norms, and the household may be larger than the one they managed abroad. A household staff provider can help by:

  • Explaining current expectations for duties, hours and pay.
  • Suggesting a sensible mix of staff for the new home.
  • Finding workers comfortable with the family's preferred routines and language.
  • Arranging trial periods so everyone can adjust gradually.

Day-to-Day Management Tips for International Employers

  • Write routines in simple English and, if possible, in Urdu as well. A bilingual checklist avoids misunderstandings.
  • Hold a short weekly meeting with your housekeeper or main staff member to plan events, guests and travel.
  • Agree on a communication channel, such as WhatsApp, for quick updates and photos of tasks if helpful.
  • Plan for power cuts and water timings, and make sure staff know how to operate the UPS, generator or water motor.
  • Keep emergency contacts visible, including your organisation's security office, the nearest hospital and Rescue 1122.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a household staff provider in Islamabad find English-speaking staff?

Yes. Many workers have experience in international households and speak basic or good English. Mention the level you need so the provider can screen candidates accordingly.

Do household staff need police verification to work in the Diplomatic Enclave?

Police verification and specific documents are commonly required for secure areas. Check your embassy's or organisation's requirements and share them with your provider early.

Can cooks in Islamabad prepare continental food?

Many experienced cooks can prepare continental dishes, baking and salads as well as Pakistani food. Ask for a cooking trial to see their skills before hiring.

What happens to my staff when my posting ends?

A good provider can help place your staff with an incoming family or another suitable household. Writing a detailed reference also helps them find their next role.

Will Muslim staff handle pork or alcohol?

Many will not, for religious reasons. Discuss this openly before hiring so expectations are clear on both sides.
