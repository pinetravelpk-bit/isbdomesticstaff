A live in maid in Islamabad does more than clean. She becomes part of the daily rhythm of the house: there before breakfast, around when children come home from school and available when an elderly parent needs a hand in the evening. That closeness is exactly why live-in arrangements need more thought than any other kind of domestic help. This guide covers who a live-in maid suits, how to prepare your home, how to set fair working and off-duty hours, what she needs to feel safe and respected, and how to make the arrangement last for years rather than weeks.

What a Live-In Arrangement Really Means

A live-in maid stays in your home, usually in her own room, and works for the family during agreed hours. She typically:

  • Lives with the family six days a week, with a weekly day off
  • Works a defined daily schedule, with breaks and off-duty time
  • Receives accommodation and meals as part of the arrangement
  • Visits her own family on her days off or during agreed leave

The most important point is that living in the house does not mean working all the time. A good live-in arrangement has clear boundaries between work and rest, just like any other job.

Who a Live-In Maid Suits

A live-in maid in Islamabad is often the best option for:

  • Large homes where the workload spreads across the whole day
  • Families with young children who need help early in the morning and in the evening
  • Homes with elderly parents who benefit from someone being nearby
  • Working couples with long or unpredictable hours, such as doctors, bankers and business owners
  • Households in outer housing societies, such as parts of Bahria Town, DHA Phase II, Gulberg Greens or B-17, where daily travel for a day-shift maid is difficult

If you mainly need daytime cleaning and laundry and value privacy in the evenings, a full time maid in Islamabad may suit you better. For lighter needs, consider a part time maid in Islamabad.

Benefits and Trade-Offs

Benefits

  • Help is available at the times families need it most: mornings and evenings
  • No dependence on daily transport, weather or road closures
  • Continuity and a closer bond with children and elderly members
  • Easier to manage a large home with a steady routine

Trade-offs

  • Less privacy for the family
  • The need for a suitable private room and facilities
  • Responsibility for her meals, wellbeing and safety while she lives with you
  • The risk of overworking her if boundaries are not respected

Thinking honestly about these trade-offs before hiring prevents disappointment on both sides.

Preparing Your Home

Before a live-in maid arrives, make sure the basics are in place. Agencies and experienced maids look closely at living conditions, and good facilities help attract and keep reliable staff.

  • A private room with a door that locks from the inside
  • A proper bed, mattress, pillow, sheets and blankets
  • A fan for summer and a heater or extra bedding for winter, since Islamabad winters can be cold
  • Access to a bathroom, ideally separate or with clear shared timings
  • Storage for clothes and personal belongings
  • A light, a power socket for her phone and adequate ventilation
Ask yourself: Would you be comfortable sleeping in this room for a year? If not, improve it before she arrives. A decent room is one of the strongest reasons good live-in maids stay.

Setting Working Hours and Off-Duty Time

Unclear hours are the single biggest cause of conflict in live-in arrangements. Write down and agree on:

  • Start and finish time for the working day
  • A lunch break and an afternoon rest, especially in summer
  • Evening off-duty time, after which she is not expected to work except in emergencies
  • The weekly day off, and whether she spends it at home or visits family
  • How late-night requests are handled, for example guests or a sick child

Families sometimes say "she lives here anyway" when asking for one more cup of tea at eleven at night. Occasional flexibility is fine, but if it becomes routine, she is effectively working around the clock. Respecting off-duty time is what separates a fair live-in job from an exhausting one.

Food, Phone and Personal Freedom

Meals

Provide regular, adequate meals, ideally the same food the family eats, or agree on a clear food arrangement. Make sure she has access to drinking water, tea and snacks during the day.

Phone and communication

She should keep her own phone and be able to contact her family regularly. Reasonable rules on phone use during working hours are fine, but taking her phone away is not.

Documents

Never keep her CNIC or personal documents against her will. A copy for your records is sufficient. Holding original documents creates distrust and is not acceptable practice.

Going out and visits home

Agree on how she will spend her day off, how often she will visit family, and how she will travel. Many families help with transport for longer journeys.

Safety for Everyone

Living together means safety goes both ways. Your family needs to trust the maid, and she needs to feel safe in your home.

Protecting your family

  • Verify her CNIC, address and family contacts
  • Speak to previous employers
  • Arrange police verification, which is strongly recommended for every live-in placement
  • Start with a trial period before final commitment
  • Keep valuables and cash secured

Protecting the maid

  • Ensure every household member, driver, guard and guest treats her respectfully
  • Give her a lockable room and personal privacy
  • Avoid situations where she feels uncomfortable, such as being left alone for long periods with male staff she does not know
  • Make sure she knows how to contact the agency if she has a concern

Typical Duties

Duties depend on your household, but a live-in maid in Islamabad commonly handles:

  • Daily cleaning of all rooms, bathrooms and kitchen
  • Laundry, ironing and folding
  • Washing dishes throughout the day
  • Kitchen help, such as preparing vegetables and serving meals
  • Light support with children's routines
  • Keeping elderly family members company and helping with small tasks

Be careful not to combine too many roles. Full cooking for a large family, constant care of a newborn or personal care for a bedridden elderly person are each demanding jobs on their own. If you need these, consider a cook, nanny or trained attendant alongside the maid.

Salary and the Full Package

Salaries for live-in maids vary with experience, duties, family size and location. When comparing offers, remember that accommodation and meals are part of the package, but they do not replace a fair cash salary. Agree on:

  • Monthly salary and a fixed payment date
  • Eid bonus and any other customary gifts
  • Annual leave, including time to visit her family at Eid
  • Help with travel costs for visits home, if agreed
  • A periodic increase for continued good service

Pay on time every month. For live-in maids, who often send money home, late salary causes real hardship and is a leading reason for sudden departures.

Leave, Eid and Family Travel

Plan leave together and early. Eid is when most live-in maids want to go home, which is also when many families host guests. Discuss dates well in advance and confirm her return date before she leaves.

When the family travels, decide whether she will take her annual leave at the same time or stay to look after the house. If she stays, leave clear instructions, money for essentials, a trusted contact and continue her salary as normal.

The First Month

The first few weeks set the tone for the whole placement:

  1. Show her the house, her room, the bathroom she will use and where supplies are kept.
  2. Explain the routine and give a written list of daily and weekly duties.
  3. Introduce the family, including children and elderly members, and explain who gives instructions.
  4. Check in daily at first, then weekly, to adjust anything that is not working.
  5. Watch for homesickness. Many maids miss their families in the first weeks. A phone call home and a little understanding help.

Warning Signs to Address Early

Watch for signs that the arrangement is under strain:

  • She seems constantly tired or unwell
  • Work is being done later and later into the night
  • She becomes withdrawn or avoids talking
  • She asks repeatedly to go home
  • Small mistakes increase suddenly

These usually point to overwork, homesickness or a problem in the household. A calm conversation, a review of hours and, if needed, help from the agency can often fix things before she decides to leave.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I provide for a live in maid in Islamabad?

A private, lockable room with a proper bed, bedding, a fan and heating, access to a bathroom, regular meals, the freedom to use her phone and clear off-duty time.

How many hours should a live-in maid work?

Agree on a defined working day with breaks and evening off-duty time. Living in the house should not mean being on call at all hours.

Is police verification necessary for a live-in maid?

It is strongly recommended for every live-in placement, together with CNIC, address and reference checks.

Can I keep my live-in maid's CNIC for safety?

No. Keep a copy for your records, but the original document should stay with her.
