Domestic Help Islamabad

19 November 2026 · 8 min read · ISB Domestic Staff Team

Most articles about domestic help in Islamabad are written from the employer's side: how to find someone, what to check, what to pay. This one looks from the other side of the door. Who are the people who cook, clean, drive and care for families across the capital? What challenges do they face, what makes them stay with a family for years, and what makes them leave? Understanding domestic help as people, not just as a service, is the most reliable way to hire well and keep good help for the long term.

Who Provides Domestic Help in Islamabad?

Domestic workers in Islamabad and Rawalpindi come from many backgrounds. Typical profiles include:

  • Women from nearby urban settlements such as those around Rawalpindi, Bhara Kahu, Tarlai and Golra, who work part-time in several homes to support their families.
  • Migrants from other parts of Pakistan, including southern Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir, who live in for long periods and send money home.
  • Experienced cooks and drivers who have worked for years in large households, offices or international homes.
  • Patient attendants who have learned care skills through hospital work, family caregiving or short training courses.
  • Couples where one partner works as a maid or cook and the other as a driver, gardener or watchman in the same household.

Many have years of experience but little formal education. Many are supporting children, elderly parents or siblings. Recognising this helps you understand their priorities.

What Domestic Help Values Most in an Employer

When domestic workers explain why they stayed with a family for many years, the same themes come up again and again:

  1. Salary on time. For a worker supporting a family, a delayed salary can mean unpaid rent or school fees. Reliability matters more than many employers realise.
  2. Respect. Being spoken to politely, by adults and children, is often mentioned before pay.
  3. Reasonable hours. Workers who are expected to be available from dawn to midnight burn out quickly.
  4. Clear duties. Knowing what is expected, without constant new tasks added without discussion.
  5. Help in emergencies. An advance for a medical bill or flexibility during a family crisis builds deep loyalty.
  6. Decent food and living space. For live-in help, a clean room and the same food quality as the household's everyday meals matter a great deal.
  7. Trust. Workers notice when they are treated with constant suspicion, and it affects morale.

The Challenges Domestic Workers Face

Understanding the pressures on domestic help explains many behaviours that frustrate employers:

Long commutes

Live-out workers may travel from Rawalpindi or the outskirts of Islamabad by public transport or shared vans, sometimes changing vehicles twice. Late arrivals are often caused by transport, not carelessness. Matching workers who live closer, or offering a small travel allowance, can reduce the problem.

Distance from family

Live-in workers may see their own children only a few times a year. This is why Eid leave is so important, and why some workers return late after holidays.

Uncertain income

Part-time workers who lose one household can lose a large part of their income overnight. This sometimes makes them accept more homes than they can manage, which affects quality.

Health

Physical work, long hours and limited access to healthcare take a toll. Back pain, untreated illnesses and exhaustion are common.

Lack of formal protection

Many domestic workers have no written contract and little recourse if they are treated unfairly. This is changing slowly, and responsible employers and agencies are leading the way.

A simple truth: The families who keep good domestic help for ten or fifteen years are rarely the ones who pay the most. They are the ones who are consistent, fair and kind.

A Typical Day for Part-Time Domestic Help in Islamabad

To understand the pressures, it helps to picture an ordinary day. The example below is illustrative, based on routines that many part-time workers describe.

  • 5:30 am: wakes up, prepares breakfast for her own children and gets them ready for school.
  • 7:00 am: leaves home on the outskirts of Rawalpindi, changing between a wagon and the Metro to reach Islamabad.
  • 8:30 am – 11:00 am: first house in G-11: sweeping, mopping, dishes and laundry.
  • 11:15 am – 1:30 pm: second house nearby: cleaning bathrooms, ironing and kitchen work.
  • 2:00 pm – 4:00 pm: third house in F-10, often with extra tasks if guests are expected.
  • 4:30 pm – 6:00 pm: long journey home, then cooking, cleaning and caring for her own family.

Seen this way, a delayed salary, an unplanned extra task or a harsh word carries far more weight. It also explains why workers value employers who are organised, predictable and considerate.

Part-Time Helpers Who Work in Several Homes

Much of the domestic help in Islamabad is part-time, with one person serving two, three or even four households a day. This arrangement suits many smaller homes, but it works best when employers keep a few points in mind:

  • Respect the agreed time slot. If your helper is due at another home by noon, keeping her late affects her other employer and her income.
  • Plan tasks in advance. A short list of priorities for the day helps her finish efficiently.
  • Give notice of changes. If you will be travelling or no longer need help on certain days, tell her early so she can arrange other work.
  • Pay for the agreed days, even if you cancel at short notice, where that has been agreed.

Part-time workers who feel secure in their arrangements are far more reliable and far less likely to drop a household suddenly.

Why Good Domestic Help Leaves

From the worker's side, these are the most common reasons for quitting:

  • Salary delayed or cut without explanation.
  • Workload gradually increasing without any change in pay.
  • Harsh words, shouting or humiliation, especially in front of others.
  • Being accused of theft without evidence.
  • No proper rest, weekly off or leave.
  • Children of the household behaving disrespectfully without correction.
  • A better offer elsewhere, often from a family known for treating staff well.

Warning signs often appear weeks before a worker resigns: quieter behaviour, more frequent absences, or questions about salary and leave. A calm, private conversation at that stage, asking whether anything is troubling them, can save a relationship that would otherwise end suddenly.

Almost all of these are within the employer's control. That is good news: it means retention is something you can actively improve.

How Understanding Domestic Help Improves Your Hiring

Write a realistic job description

Workers often accept jobs and leave within weeks because the role turned out to be different from what was described. Be honest about the size of the house, the number of people and the hours.

Offer a fair, competitive salary

Ask a domestic help provider for current market rates in Islamabad for the role and hours you need. Paying a little above average for an excellent worker is usually cheaper than repeatedly replacing someone who left for a better offer.

Consider commute and living conditions

For live-out help, ask where the candidate lives and how they will travel. For live-in help, show them the room before they decide.

Interview with respect

The interview is the worker's first impression of your household. Offer a seat and a glass of water, explain the job clearly and listen to their questions. Good workers are also choosing their employers.

Agree on terms in writing

A simple written agreement protects the worker as much as it protects you, and serious candidates appreciate it.

Supporting Your Domestic Help Beyond Salary

Small gestures often matter more than their cost suggests:

  • Eid bonus: customary in Pakistan and deeply appreciated.
  • Winter clothing or bedding for live-in staff during Islamabad's cold months.
  • Help with children's education, such as school fees or books, for long-serving workers.
  • Medical support, such as covering a doctor's visit or medicines.
  • Skills development, like teaching new recipes or basic first aid, which increases the worker's value and confidence.
  • A reference letter when the arrangement ends, which can shape their future employment.

Domestic Help and Children: Teaching Respect at Home

Children learn how to treat others by watching adults. Families in Islamabad who want their children to grow up respectful often make a point of:

  • Teaching children to say please and thank you to household staff.
  • Correcting rude behaviour immediately and privately.
  • Not allowing children to give orders in a demanding tone.
  • Including staff in small celebrations, such as sharing birthday cake.

This creates a happier home and makes domestic help far more likely to stay. It also teaches children a lesson in dignity and fairness that will serve them throughout their lives, long after they have grown up.

The Role of a Responsible Domestic Help Provider

A good provider serves both sides. For families, it verifies identity and references, matches skills and offers replacements. For workers, it should ensure fair salaries, honest job descriptions, reasonable hours and safe placements, and it should never place children in domestic work. When both sides are treated fairly, placements last longer and everyone benefits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I find reliable domestic help in Islamabad?

Use a verified provider that checks identity and references, describe the job honestly, offer a fair salary and agree on terms in writing. Interviews and a trial day help confirm the fit.

Why does my domestic help keep leaving?

Common reasons include late salaries, growing workloads without extra pay, long hours, disrespectful treatment and long commutes. Addressing these usually improves retention significantly.

Is it customary to give domestic help an Eid bonus?

Yes. An Eid bonus is a widely expected custom in Pakistan and is greatly appreciated by domestic workers.

Should I help my domestic worker with medical expenses?

Many good employers help with medical costs or offer an advance during emergencies. It is not always required, but it builds trust and loyalty.
