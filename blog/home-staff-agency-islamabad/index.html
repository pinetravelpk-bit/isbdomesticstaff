Home Staff Agency Islamabad

18 November 2026 · 8 min read · ISB Domestic Staff Team

Families comparing a home staff agency in Islamabad usually focus on one question: "Can you send someone good?" An equally important question is often skipped: "What exactly am I paying for, and what happens if it goes wrong?" Fees, written agreements and replacement guarantees are where many disappointments begin. This guide explains how agencies typically charge, what a fair agreement contains, how replacement guarantees really work, and how to compare offers so you choose an agency that is transparent from the start.

Why the Business Side Matters

Two agencies can send equally capable workers, yet one leaves you satisfied and the other leaves you frustrated. The difference is usually clarity: how fees were explained, whether terms were written down, and whether the agency honoured its promises when a worker left suddenly. Understanding the business side of a home staff agency in Islamabad protects your money and your peace of mind.

How Home Staff Agencies Usually Charge

There is no single pricing model in Pakistan, but most agencies use one of the following approaches or a mix of them.

ModelHow it worksWhat to check
One-time placement feeThe family pays a fee once a worker is selected and starts.Does the fee include replacements, and for how long?
Registration or search feeA smaller amount paid upfront to begin the search, sometimes adjusted against the final fee.Is it refundable if no suitable candidate is found?
Monthly service chargeThe agency charges a monthly amount while the worker is employed, often with ongoing support.What support do you receive for the monthly charge?
Deduction from worker's salaryThe agency takes a share of the worker's pay.Is this disclosed to the worker? Is the worker still paid fairly?

Exact amounts vary with the role, experience level, urgency and whether the worker is live-in. Rather than relying on numbers seen online, ask each agency for a written quote for your specific requirement.

Fairness check: Be cautious of arrangements where an agency quietly takes a large share of the worker's salary. Underpaid workers are more likely to leave, and the disruption ultimately falls on your family.

Questions to Ask About Fees

  1. What is the total amount I will pay, and when?
  2. Is anything payable before I meet candidates?
  3. What does the fee include: verification, police checks, replacements, follow-up?
  4. If I do not find a suitable person, is any amount refunded?
  5. Are there extra charges for urgent placements, live-in staff or specialised roles such as patient attendants?
  6. Will I receive a written receipt for every payment?

A trustworthy home staff agency in Islamabad will answer all of these clearly and put the answers in writing.

Understanding Replacement Guarantees

"Replacement guarantee" is one of the most common promises in domestic staffing, and one of the most misunderstood. Before you rely on it, clarify the details.

Duration

How long after placement does the guarantee last? Some agencies offer a set number of weeks or months. Ask whether the period restarts with each replacement.

Number of replacements

Is it one replacement, a fixed number, or unlimited within the period?

What triggers a replacement

Typical reasons include the worker leaving without notice, poor performance, or a mismatch with the family. Ask whether a replacement is still provided if you end the arrangement, not just if the worker leaves.

How quickly

Ask how soon a replacement is usually arranged for your role. For essential roles such as nannies and patient attendants, ask whether temporary cover is available in the meantime.

Conditions on the family

Some guarantees require the family to have paid the worker correctly and followed the agreed duties and hours. This is reasonable; just make sure you know the conditions in advance.

What a Fair Written Agreement Should Include

You do not need a complicated legal contract, but a simple written agreement between you, the worker and the agency avoids most disputes. It should cover:

  • Names and CNIC numbers of the employer and the worker, and the agency's details.
  • Job title and duties, including what is not part of the job.
  • Working hours, breaks and expectations about evenings or guests.
  • Salary, payment date and method, plus any allowances.
  • Weekly off and annual leave, including the Eid arrangement.
  • Accommodation and meals for live-in staff.
  • Trial period and what happens at the end of it.
  • Notice period for both sides.
  • Agency fee and replacement terms.
  • Signatures of all parties, with copies for each.

Keep your copy with the worker's CNIC copy and photograph. If you ever need the agency's help, these documents make the conversation straightforward.

It is also worth keeping a simple monthly record of salary payments, advances and leave taken, signed or acknowledged by the worker. A notebook or a shared note on your phone is enough. When questions arise months later, a clear record settles them in minutes and keeps the relationship friendly.

Advances, Loans and Salary Disputes

Requests for salary advances are common in domestic employment, often for family emergencies, weddings or medical bills. Handled well, an advance builds loyalty; handled badly, it causes disputes.

  • Agree on advances in writing, with a clear repayment schedule.
  • Keep deductions reasonable so the worker still has enough to live on.
  • Inform the agency about large advances, so there is a record if the worker leaves.
  • Never hold a worker's original CNIC or documents as security. It is unfair and can create serious problems.

If a salary disagreement arises, involve the agency early. A neutral party can usually resolve misunderstandings before they turn into a sudden resignation.

Special Terms for Live-In and Specialised Roles

Some roles need extra points in the agreement:

  • Live-in staff: describe the room, bathroom access, meals, rest hours at night, and whether the worker may leave the house on their off day. Agree on what happens if the family travels.
  • Patient attendants: list the patient's needs clearly, including lifting, night duty and hospital visits, and confirm that clinical tasks are handled by a nurse.
  • Nannies: specify the children's ages, routines, outings allowed, screen-time rules and whether travel with the family is expected.
  • Drivers: agree on working hours, overtime, out-of-city trips, responsibility for fuel records and what happens with traffic fines.
  • Watchmen: define shift timings, rounds, visitor logs and whom to call in an emergency.

A good home staff agency in Islamabad will help you add these points so nothing important is left to assumption.

Ending an Arrangement the Right Way

Even successful placements come to an end, whether because a family relocates, children grow up or the worker returns to their village. Ending things properly protects your reputation and keeps the door open for future help.

  1. Give the agreed notice, or pay in lieu of notice if you need the worker to leave immediately.
  2. Settle all dues: salary for days worked, any agreed bonus and a fair arrangement for outstanding advances.
  3. Return personal belongings and documents promptly.
  4. Inform the agency, so its records are updated and the worker can be placed again.
  5. Provide a short reference if the worker served well. It helps them find their next job and strengthens the whole system of trusted references.

Red Flags in Pricing and Paperwork

  • Full payment demanded before you meet a single candidate.
  • Verbal promises with nothing in writing.
  • No receipts for payments.
  • Vague answers about replacements: "Don't worry, we will manage."
  • Pressure to sign quickly because "the candidate has other offers."
  • Requests that you hold the worker's CNIC or pay their salary through the agency without explanation.

Getting the Best Value From Your Agency

Once you have chosen an agency, a few habits help you get the most from the relationship:

  • Give a detailed brief so the first shortlist is accurate and you avoid repeat searches.
  • Interview promptly; good candidates are often placed quickly.
  • Report problems early, while you are still within the guarantee period.
  • Stay with one trusted agency for future needs. Agencies usually prioritise families they know treat staff well.
  • Keep your documents organised so replacements and follow-ups are quick.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a home staff agency in Islamabad charge?

Charges depend on the role, experience, urgency and whether the worker lives in. Most agencies charge a placement or service fee; ask for a written quote that lists exactly what is included.

Should I pay the agency before meeting candidates?

A small registration fee may be reasonable at some agencies, but you should not pay the full amount before meeting suitable candidates. Always get a receipt.

What does a replacement guarantee usually cover?

It typically covers a worker leaving or not meeting expectations within a set period. Check the duration, the number of replacements and how quickly they are arranged.

Do I need a written contract with domestic staff?

A simple written agreement covering duties, hours, salary, leave and notice is strongly recommended. It protects both the family and the worker and makes disputes easier to resolve.

Is it acceptable to keep a worker's CNIC as security?

No. Keep a photocopy for your records, but the original CNIC belongs to the worker and should never be held by the employer.
